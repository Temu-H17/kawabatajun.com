Research

Research

My goal is to understand the relationships among protein evolution, stability, and function and use that knowledge to design more useful enzymes.

Undergraduate thesis

Ancestral protein reconstruction

I collect homologous sequences, perform multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic inference, and analyze ancestral sequence features using residues conserved and supported across phylogenetic groups.

Engineering PET-degrading enzymes

Using BhrPETase as a model enzyme, I design candidate mutations aimed at improving stability and function and evaluate synthesized genes and variants experimentally.

Computational workflow

BLASTPMAFFTtrimAlFastTreeIQ-TREE 2UFBootPyMOLFoldXAlphaFold

My workflow connects sequence search → multiple alignment → trimming → phylogenetic inference → candidate mutation selection → structural analysis with experimental validation.

Experimental work

I perform PCR, restriction digestion, ligation, transformation using DH5α, colony screening, miniprep, sequence verification, protein expression, and SDS-PAGE.

Next step

From April 2027, I plan to continue research on protein structure and function in graduate school, focusing on PET-degrading enzymes.
